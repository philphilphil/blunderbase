Move list layouts

Five ways to space the move table, side by side on your game from the #45 screenshot (10+0, 18 moves). They share one cursor: click a move in any of them, or use the arrow keys, and every list follows. The slider sets the column width for all of them.

White's clock sits next to Black's move

The clock is right-aligned at the end of White's half, which puts it beside the next move. In the screenshot, 9:58 is about 11 px from Nf6 and about 77 px from d4, so it reads as Black's.

d4 ! ≈77 px 9:58 ≈11 Nf6

The cells stretch with the column

Both move cells are flex-1, so a wider track pushes a move, its badge and its clock further apart rather than leaving the room at the end. Drag the slider on “Today” to watch it happen.

Three highlights at once

Boxed badges on half the moves, a tinted row for the pair, and a boxed current move whose box runs the whole width of its cell. Each is right on its own; together the row is busy.

A

Fixed columns

CSS only

The same table on a fixed grid. Each clock sits after its own move, and spare width stays at the end of the row.

  • Row becomes grid with columns 1.75 · 6 · 2.25 · 1 · 6 · 2.25 rem, capped at 20rem.
  • The move button hugs its text, so the current-move box is the size of the move.
  • Numbers right-aligned, so 9. and 10. line up on the dot.
B

Ledger

CSS + highlight rules

Lichess's shape: a shaded number gutter and two cells per row, each cell holding its move and its clock.

  • A hairline between the halves groups each clock with its move.
  • The whole cell is the button and lights up; the pair shows as an accent number instead of a tinted row.
  • Glyphs as coloured text, rows 1.5rem: about 15 % shorter.
C

Think time

CSS + derived data

A's grid, but the clock column shows how long each move took, as a number and a small bar. The clock as it stood is in the tooltip.

  • Long thinks (top tenth of the game) are blue, so the hard moments show at a glance.
  • Under 20 s left, the remaining clock replaces the think time, in orange.
  • Changes what the column means; the Move time graph already says some of this.
D

Fixed and calm

my pick · CSS + glyph text

A's columns with B's quieter details: glyphs as coloured text and 1.5rem rows. Row tint and selected move stay as the app's grammar has them.

  • Everything in A.
  • ClassificationBadge becomes a coloured suffix (??, ?!) in the move list only.
  • Rows h-6 instead of h-7.

My pick: D

It fixes the clock that reads as the opponent's, stops the row from stretching on wide columns, and calms the row down, without changing what the table is. It keeps the clock, the row tint and the selected-move box exactly as the app's design grammar defines them.

A is the smallest step if you want to change only the spacing. B looks best on a wide column but bends the highlight rules. C is a different feature: I'd offer it as a setting, not as the default.

Separate from layout: try the ! switch above. Without it, the flagged moves are the only glyphs left, and they stand out much more.

What D takes in MoveList.tsx

  • Row: grid h-6 max-w-[20rem] grid-cols-[1.75rem_minmax(0,6rem)_2.25rem_1rem_minmax(0,6rem)_2.25rem] instead of flex h-7; a 1rem spacer cell before Black.
  • Move button: justify-self-start instead of flex-1.
  • Number: text-right pr-1.5.
  • Glyph: a coloured suffix span from GLYPHS[glyph].textClass instead of ClassificationBadge.
  • Variation and “Best was …” indent: pl-[2rem], so they start under the moves.
  • Tests: the clock cells keep their data-testid; no behaviour changes.

Clocks, glyphs and note marks are from the game in the #45 screenshot. “Best was …” lines come from Stockfish on this machine, at 1.5M nodes per position. The 3+0 clocks are made up, to show time trouble.
